Research

Two pieces of research training, each described by its task, approach and outcome.

Toxicological effects of biorational insecticides on Pieris brassicae

2015 – 2017 at the University of Agriculture, Faisalabad

Task

Evaluated how biorational insecticides affect the immune response of cabbage butterfly larvae. The work addressed a gap in understanding the non-target physiological effects of insecticides marketed as "safer" alternatives.

Approach

Designed and ran controlled laboratory bioassays exposing larvae to graded insecticide doses. I then analysed haemocyte counts and morphology under microscopy, using statistical comparison to identify dose-dependent immune responses.

Outcome

The findings clarified how biorational insecticides affect insect immune cells. They contribute evidence for choosing insecticides that control pests effectively while minimising disruption to non-target physiological processes.

Study design

  1. Cabbage butterfly larvae exposed to graded insecticide doses
  2. Haemocyte counts recorded under the microscope
  3. Haemocyte morphology analysed
  4. Statistical comparison to identify dose-dependent immune responses

Evaluation of Toxicological Responses of Biorational Insecticides on Haemocytes of Cabbage Butterfly, Pieris brassicae L. (Lepidoptera: Pieridae), under Laboratory Conditions.

Thesis title. The findings informed insecticide-selection recommendations for Lepidoptera-focused IPM programmes.

Task

Trained in biological control methods to strengthen non-chemical approaches within IPM programme design.

Approach

Studied host–parasitoid interactions and natural enemy population dynamics, and assessed how compatible pesticide use is with conserving beneficial insect populations.

Outcome

Built a working understanding of how to integrate biological control agents into IPM strategy, which I later applied when designing commercial pest-management programmes.

What the training covered

  1. Host–parasitoid interactions
  2. Natural enemy population dynamics
  3. Compatibility of pesticide use with beneficial insects
  4. Integrating biological control agents into IPM strategy
